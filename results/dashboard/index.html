Co-evolutionary malware detection

Which retraining policy wins the iterated game?

A controlled factorial comparison of defensive retraining policies under sustained adversarial pressure, measured on the real EMBER-2018 corpus. Every policy runs through one harness and is scored on identical metrics, so differences are attributable to the policy variable rather than to a bundled defence mechanism.

06

Secondary findings

Results that were not the target of the study but fall out of it, and that a reviewer familiar with this literature will ask about.

01

Hardest-example mining is the worst strategy, not the best

Mining only the deepest evasions scores 0.351 settled evasion — worse than a plain FIFO buffer (0.294) and 6.4× worse than full replay (0.055) — and oscillates most (0.046). Selecting for depth discards the diversity the defender needs in order to generalise.

02

Threshold-triggered cadence only pays off if the data strategy works

With full replay the trigger fires 3 times out of 8 because evasion is actually driven below it. With the weaker strategies evasion never falls below the trigger, so the policy fires every round and silently degenerates into retrain-always at full cost.

03

Oscillation tracks robustness rather than trading against it

The policies that settle lowest also swing least: full replay pairs 0.055 evasion with 0.011 oscillation, hard mining pairs 0.351 with 0.046. Nothing here shows a policy buying stability at the price of robustness, which supports the oscillation index as a diagnostic rather than a competing objective.

04

Robustness was not bought with clean accuracy

Clean accuracy stays between 0.980 and 0.984 across all eight rounds of the representative run, so the defender is not trading false positives for adversarial robustness.

07

Method & limits

What this measurement is, and what it is not. Stated up front because two of these constraints materially bound the claims.

▸

Feature-space attack, not raw binaries

The attacker perturbs EMBER feature vectors, not PE files. A raw-binary attacker needs a malware corpus under institutional sign-off and VM isolation. The feature-space formulation is standard in this literature, but the evasion rates here are not measured against a functioning executable.

▸

The mutability assumption decides the outcome if set wrongly

The attacker may edit every feature a functionality-preserving modification actually affects; only the 40 structural header dimensions are frozen. This is not a detail: with general, datadirectories and exports also frozen, a classifier trained on the frozen remainder alone reaches 0.970 accuracy, retraining simply learns those features, and every policy ties at zero evasion.

▸

Stochastic mimicry attacker; RL agents were compared and rejected

DQN and PPO are implemented but apply weaker and less stable pressure at any feasible budget (round-0 attack success 0.018 for DQN against 0.783 here), so the study uses the stronger attacker and reports the comparison as a secondary result.
